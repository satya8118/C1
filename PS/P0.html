§1 Overview

Each student is assigned one function from the bank below — 50 function templates, reused across 3 groups of 50 students, with a different parameter value per student. You will work with this same function throughout the semester, applying each new concept from the course to it as soon as it is taught.

The project is submitted in five phases aligned with the course syllabus, and compiled into one final portfolio at the end of the semester. The project is worth 25 marks in total — see the rubric in §4.

§2 Ground rules

  1. Your function and parameter value are assigned by roll number — see the allocation table in §6. Do not change your function or parameter without written permission.
  2. Every claim (domain, one-one, continuity, etc.) must be justified with a short proof or clearly shown computation — a bare answer earns no credit.
  3. Use Desmos for quick graphical exploration and verification; use Python (matplotlib, numpy, scipy) for at least one numerical or plotted verification per phase, as specified. Include the plot and a one-line caption.
  4. Some functions (especially #35–#41 and #48–#50) do not have elementary antiderivatives, or involve limits/asymptotes that need care — where a computation cannot be done by hand, say so explicitly and evaluate it numerically instead. This is expected, not a shortcoming.
  5. Each phase is submitted (handwritten or typed, plus your Desmos link and Python plots/code) by the deadline announced in class; late phases lose 10% per day.
  6. At the end of the semester, compile all five phases into a single portfolio document with a one-page summary of your function's complete behaviour.

§3 Task list, by phase

Weeks 1-3

Phase 1 — Getting to know your function

  1. State the domain and range of \(f\), with full justification.
  2. Locate all zeros of \(f\) (solve \(f(x)=0\)), and determine the sign of \(f\) on each interval of its domain.
  3. Sketch the graph of \(f\) by hand, then verify it on Desmos. Mark intercepts, asymptotes, and any restricted domain.
  4. Produce and interpret the graphs of the shifts \(f(x+c)\), \(f(x)+c\), \(f(cx)\), \(c\cdot f(x)\), \(-f(x)\), \(f(-x)\) for at least two values of \(c\) each (Desmos or matplotlib).
Weeks 4–6

Phase 2 — Structure of the function

  1. Determine whether \(f\) is one-one on its full domain. If not, identify the largest interval containing a given point on which it is one-one.
  2. On that interval, find the inverse \(f^{-1}\) and state its domain and range. Graph \(f\) and \(f^{-1}\) together with the line \(y=x\).
  3. Determine whether \(f\) is onto its codomain (\(\mathbb{R}\), or the interval you take as codomain). Restate as a bijection onto its range where needed.
  4. Compute and simplify, wherever defined: \(f(x)+f^{-1}(x)\), \((f+f)(x)=2f(x)\), \((f\cdot f)(x)=f(x)^2\), and \((f\circ f)(x)\). Comment on how the domain changes under each combination.
Weeks 6–9

Phase 3 — Continuity and differentiability

  1. Prove continuity of \(f\) at a generic interior point of its domain, and examine continuity at any boundary/singular point (e.g. where the domain has a hole or the definition changes).
  2. Determine where \(f\) is differentiable; identify and justify any point of non-differentiability.
  3. Compute \(f'(x)\) and find the equation of the tangent line to the graph at a specified point \(x=x_0\) (instructor- or self-chosen). Plot the curve and its tangent together.
  4. Discuss monotonicity: find the intervals on which \(f\) is increasing / decreasing using the sign of \(f'\).
Weeks 9–11

Phase 4 — Extrema and shape of the graph

  1. Find all local extrema (first- and second-derivative tests) and determine whether \(f\) has a global maximum and/or minimum on its domain.
  2. Find \(f''(x)\); determine the intervals on which \(f\) is concave and convex, and locate all inflection points.
  3. Compute the curvature \(\kappa(x) = \dfrac{|f''(x)|}{(1+f'(x)^2)^{3/2}}\) of the graph and evaluate it at the same point \(x_0\) used for the tangent line.
  4. Summarise Phases 3–4 in a single annotated graph: mark extrema, inflection points, concave/convex intervals, and the tangent at \(x_0\).
Weeks 12–13

Phase 5 — Integration and arc length

  1. Find an antiderivative \(F(x)\) of \(f\), by hand where elementary; where it is not elementary in closed form, say so explicitly and evaluate it numerically instead.
  2. Evaluate a definite integral of \(f\) over a meaningful interval from your domain analysis (e.g. between two zeros, or over one period).
  3. Set up the arc-length integral \(\displaystyle\int \sqrt{1+f'(x)^2}\,dx\) over a chosen interval; evaluate it exactly if possible, otherwise numerically (scipy.integrate.quad or Desmos).
  4. Use Python (matplotlib/numpy/scipy) to reproduce your key graphs (function, tangent, shifts) and to numerically verify at least one hand computation (a root, an extremum, or the definite integral).

§5 Function bank

Each row is a template with one parameter \(a\). The same 50 templates are reused for all three groups; only the value of \(a\) assigned to each student differs (see §6), so no two students receive an identical function.

Algebraic & piecewise (1–11)

#Function family \(f(x)\)Why it works well as a semester function
1\(ax+|x|\)Piecewise-linear; slope jumps at 0 unless \(a=\pm1\) (exclude). Contrasts continuity vs differentiability.
2\(x|x-a|\)Cubic-like piecewise; corner at \(x=a\) moves with \(a\).
3\(x^3-ax\)\(a>0\) gives a local max/min pair; \(a\le 0\) is monotone — good one-one discussion.
4\(ax^2-x^4\)W- or M-shaped depending on sign of \(a\); two symmetric extrema.
5\(x+a/x\)  (\(x\ne0\))AM–GM flavour; clean min/max, vertical asymptote.
6\(1/(x^2-a^2)\)Two vertical asymptotes at \(\pm a\); forces careful domain work.
7\((x-a)/(x^2+1)\)Bounded, horizontal asymptote \(y=0\); extremum shifts with \(a\).
8\((x^2-a)/(x^2+a)\), \(a>0\)Even; horizontal asymptote \(y=1\); zeros at \(x=\pm\sqrt a\).
9\(\sqrt{x^2+a}-x\)Domain \(\mathbb{R}\); distinct asymptotic behaviour as \(x\to\pm\infty\).
10\(ax^3-3x\)Inflection always at 0; extrema exist only for \(a>0\).
11\(|x^2-a|\)Non-differentiable at \(x=\pm\sqrt a\) (\(a>0\)) — differentiability-failure case.

Exponential & logarithmic (12–22)

#Function family \(f(x)\)Why it works well as a semester function
12\(x\cdot e^{ax}\)Single critical point at \(x=-1/a\); clean extremum/inflection pair.
13\(x^2\cdot e^{-ax}\)Two critical points; local vs global max.
14\(a\cdot x\cdot e^{-x^2}\)Odd, bounded bump; good for global extrema and antiderivatives.
15\(e^{-x^2/a}\)Even Gaussian bump; inflection points at \(x=\pm\sqrt{a/2}\).
16\(\ln(x^2+a)\), \(a>0\)Domain \(\mathbb{R}\); even; min at 0; log growth for large \(x\).
17\(\ln(1+ax^2)\)Growth/curvature rate varies with \(a\).
18\(\ln x+ax\), \(x>0\)Monotone for \(a\ge0\); interior max for \(a<0\).
19\(x^a\cdot e^{-x}\), \(x>0\)Gamma-integrand shape; single hump; good for by-parts integration.
20\(e^{ax}-ax\)Always convex; minimum at \(x=0\) for \(a\ne0\).
21\(a\ln x - x\), \(x>0\)Max at \(x=a\); concave throughout.
22\(x\ln x-ax\), \(x>0\)Entropy-like; convex, single minimum.

Trigonometric & inverse trig (23–33)

#Function family \(f(x)\)Why it works well as a semester function
23\(x+a\sin x\)Monotone iff \(|a|\le1\) — an \(a\)-dependent one-one condition.
24\(a\sin x+\cos x\)\(=\sqrt{a^2+1}\sin(x+\varphi)\); amplitude/phase, \(a\)-dependent range.
25\(ax-\sin x\)Monotonicity flavour mirrors #23.
26\(\tan^{-1}(ax)\)Bounded range \((-\pi/2,\pi/2)\); odd; \(a\) controls steepness.
27\(a/(1+x^2)\)Even, bounded, max at 0; clean definite integral (arctan).
28\(x\cos(ax)\), on \([-\pi/a,\pi/a]\)Oscillation controlled by \(a\); must restrict domain for one-one part.
29\(a\cos x-x\)Monotonicity flips depending on \(a\).
30\(a\tan^{-1}x-x\)Max/min depending on sign of \(a\).
31\(\csc x-a\sin x\), \(x\in(0,\pi)\)Bounded below via AM–GM when \(a\le1\); richer than a bare \(\csc x\).
32\(x-a\tan x\), \(x\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)\)Vertical asymptotes at \(\pm\pi/2\); odd.
33\(a\sin x+x\cos x\)Mixes polynomial and trig growth; stress-tests product rule and curvature.

Oscillatory, hybrid & exotic (34–50)

Added for the larger cohort — several of these are deliberately more advanced (oscillation near a point, removable/jump discontinuities, non-elementary antiderivatives) and pair well with stronger students or bonus discussion in class.

#Function family \(f(x)\)Why it works well as a semester function
34\(x^2+ax+|x|\)Quadratic core perturbed by a corner at 0; smooth except at \(x=0\); vertex shifts with \(a\) on each side.
35\(x\sin(a/x)\), \(f(0):=0\)Continuous at 0 by the Squeeze Theorem despite infinite oscillation nearby, but not differentiable at 0 — a sharp continuity-vs-differentiability contrast.
36\(x^2\sin(a/x)\), \(f(0):=0\)Differentiable everywhere including \(x=0\) (with \(f'(0)=0\)), yet \(f'\) itself is discontinuous at 0 — a step beyond the usual Phase 3 examples.
37\(\operatorname{csch}(ax)/\sin x\), \(x\in(0,\pi)\)Blends hyperbolic decay with a trig singularity; vertical asymptotes at both ends of \((0,\pi)\); extrema/curvature need numerical work.
38\(x^{ax}\), \(x>0\)Rewrite as \(e^{ax\ln x}\); single global minimum at \(x=1/(ea)\); a genuine log-differentiation exercise, non-elementary antiderivative.
39\(a\,x^2 e^{-x}\sin x\)Damped oscillator; many local extrema on a chosen window; good repeated-integration-by-parts practice.
40\(\sin(ax^2)\)"Chirp" function — zero-spacing shrinks as \(x\) grows; even; antiderivative is a Fresnel-type integral (non-elementary).
41\(ax/(1+x^4)\)Odd, bounded, two symmetric extrema, horizontal asymptote 0 — a faster-decaying alternative to #7.
42\(\ln(\cosh(ax))\)Smooth convex "soft" version of \(a|x|\); asymptotic to \(a|x|-\ln2\); contrasts nicely with #1's genuine corner.
43\(\sinh(ax)-ax\)Odd; strictly increasing everywhere; inflection at \(x=0\) separates concave/convex regions.
44\(\tanh(ax)\)Bounded \((-1,1)\); odd; inflection at 0; horizontal asymptotes \(\pm1\); clean antiderivative \(\tfrac1a\ln\cosh(ax)\).
45\(a\,x\tanh x\)Even; asymptotically linear (\(\approx a|x|\)) for large \(|x|\), smooth near 0 — nice curvature computation.
46\(a(x-\sin x)\)Strictly increasing although \(f'\) touches 0 at isolated points \(x=2k\pi\) — subtle monotonicity argument; inflections at \(x=2k\pi\).
47\(a(1-\cos x)\)Periodic and \(\ge0\); must restrict to one period for the one-one/inverse part; zeros at \(x=2k\pi\).
48\((x^2-a^2)/(x-a)\), \(x\ne a\)Simplifies to \(x+a\); a classic removable discontinuity — define \(f(a):=2a\) to extend continuously. The only "fix the discontinuity" case in the bank.
49\(a\big(x-\lfloor x\rfloor\big)\)Bonus/challenge pick. Sawtooth — the only genuinely discontinuous family: jump discontinuities at every integer, periodic, piecewise linear. Best suited to a stronger student.
50\(a\,x\,|\sin x|\)Piecewise smooth; non-differentiable at every zero of \(\sin x\) (multiples of \(\pi\)); envelope \(a|x|\) — extrema/curvature computed piece by piece.
