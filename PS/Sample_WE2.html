PROBLEM 1

Injectivity and Monotonicity of Continuous Functions

Let $I \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ be an interval and $f: I \to \mathbb{R}$ be a continuous function. Prove that $f$ is injective if and only if $f$ is strictly monotone.

Proof ($\Leftarrow$ Direction):

Assume $f$ is strictly increasing. Let $x_1, x_2 \in I$ with $x_1 \neq x_2$. Assuming $x_1 < x_2$, strict monotonicity gives $f(x_1) < f(x_2)$, so $f(x_1) \neq f(x_2)$. Thus $f$ is injective.

Proof ($\Rightarrow$ Direction):

Suppose $f$ is continuous and injective but not strictly monotone. Then there exist $a, b, c \in I$ with $a < b < c$ such that $f(b)$ does not lie strictly between $f(a)$ and $f(c)$. By injectivity, $f(a), f(b), f(c)$ are distinct.

Hence, either $f(b) > \max\{f(a), f(c)\}$ or $f(b) < \min\{f(a), f(c)\}$. Assume $f(b) > f(a)$ and $f(b) > f(c)$. Pick $y$ such that $\max\{f(a), f(c)\} < y < f(b)$. By the Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT):

  • There exists $x_1 \in (a, b)$ such that $f(x_1) = y$.
  • There exists $x_2 \in (b, c)$ such that $f(x_2) = y$.

Since $x_1 < b < x_2$, we have $x_1 \neq x_2$ while $f(x_1) = f(x_2) = y$, contradicting injectivity.

Conclusion: A continuous function on an interval is injective if and only if it is strictly monotone. $\blacksquare$
PROBLEM 2

Interlacing Zeros via Rolle's Theorem

Suppose that $f, g: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ both have continuous derivatives and the Wronskian determinant $\phi(x) = f(x)g'(x) - f'(x)g(x)$ is never zero. Show that between any two zeros of $f$, there must be a zero of $g$.

Proof:

Let $a < b$ be consecutive zeros of $f$, so $f(a) = f(b) = 0$. Assume for contradiction that $g(x) \neq 0$ for all $x \in [a, b]$.

Define $h(x) = \frac{f(x)}{g(x)}$ on $[a, b]$. Then $h(a) = \frac{f(a)}{g(a)} = 0$ and $h(b) = \frac{f(b)}{g(b)} = 0$. Since $h$ is differentiable on $(a, b)$, Rolle's Theorem implies there exists $c \in (a, b)$ such that $h'(c) = 0$.

Calculating the derivative:

$$h'(x) = \frac{f'(x)g(x) - f(x)g'(x)}{[g(x)]^2} = -\frac{\phi(x)}{[g(x)]^2}$$

Thus $h'(c) = 0 \implies \phi(c) = 0$, which contradicts the hypothesis that $\phi(x) \neq 0$ everywhere.

Conclusion: $g(x)$ must have at least one zero between any two consecutive zeros of $f(x)$. $\blacksquare$
PROBLEM 3

Cauchy's Exponential Functional Equation

Let $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be a continuous function such that $f(1) = a > 0$. If $f(x + y) = f(x)f(y)$ for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$, find $f(x)$.

Solution:

  1. For $n \in \mathbb{N}$, induction gives $f(n) = f(1)^n = a^n$.
  2. $f(0) = f(0+0) = f(0)^2 \implies f(0) = 1$. Also $f(x)f(-x) = f(0) = 1 \implies f(-x) = \frac{1}{f(x)}$, so $f(k) = a^k$ for all $k \in \mathbb{Z}$.
  3. For rational $q = \frac{m}{n}$, $[f(q)]^n = f(nq) = f(m) = a^m \implies f(q) = a^{m/n} = a^q$.
  4. Since $\mathbb{Q}$ is dense in $\mathbb{R}$ and $f$ is continuous, for any real $x$, choosing rational $q_k \to x$ gives: $$f(x) = \lim_{k \to \infty} f(q_k) = \lim_{k \to \infty} a^{q_k} = a^x$$
Conclusion: The unique continuous solution is $f(x) = a^x$. $\blacksquare$
PROBLEM 4

Double Zeros & Rolle's Theorem

Let $f$ be a polynomial with double zeros at $a$ and $b$ ($a < b$). Show that $f'(x)$ has at least three roots in $[a, b]$.

Solution:

Since $a$ and $b$ are double zeros of $f(x)$, we have $f(a) = f'(a) = 0$ and $f(b) = f'(b) = 0$.

Applying Rolle's Theorem to $f(x)$ on $[a, b]$ (since $f(a) = f(b) = 0$), there exists $c \in (a, b)$ such that $f'(c) = 0$.

The three distinct roots of $f'(x)$ in $[a, b]$ are $x = a$, $x = c$, and $x = b$.

Conclusion: $f'(x)$ has at least three roots in $[a, b]$. $\blacksquare$
PROBLEM 5

Parallel Tangent Lines via Auxiliary Functions

Suppose $f$ and $g$ are continuous on $[a, b]$ and differentiable on $(a, b)$ with $f(a) = g(a)$ and $f(b) = g(b)$. Prove that there is $c \in (a, b)$ where the tangent line to $f$ is parallel to the tangent line to $g$.

Solution:

Define the auxiliary function $h(x) = f(x) - g(x)$.

  • $h(a) = f(a) - g(a) = 0$
  • $h(b) = f(b) - g(b) = 0$

By Rolle's Theorem, there exists $c \in (a, b)$ such that $h'(c) = 0 \implies f'(c) - g'(c) = 0 \implies f'(c) = g'(c)$. Equal derivatives mean the tangent lines at $x = c$ are parallel.

Conclusion: There exists $c \in (a, b)$ such that $f'(c) = g'(c)$. $\blacksquare$
PROBLEM 6

Second Derivative Zeros

Let $f$ be twice differentiable on $(a, b)$ and $f(x) = 0$ at three distinct points in $(a, b)$. Prove that $f''(x_0) = 0$ for some $x_0 \in (a, b)$.

Solution:

Let the three zeros be $x_1 < x_2 < x_3$.

  1. Apply Rolle's Theorem on $[x_1, x_2]$ and $[x_2, x_3]$: there exist $c_1 \in (x_1, x_2)$ and $c_2 \in (x_2, x_3)$ with $f'(c_1) = 0$ and $f'(c_2) = 0$.
  2. Apply Rolle's Theorem to $f'(x)$ on $[c_1, c_2]$: there exists $x_0 \in (c_1, c_2)$ such that $f''(x_0) = 0$.
Conclusion: $f''(x_0) = 0$ for some $x_0 \in (a, b)$. $\blacksquare$
PROBLEM 7

Strictly Increasing Functions via MVT

Prove that if $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$ and $f'(x) > 0$ on $(a, b)$, then $f$ is strictly increasing on $[a, b]$.

Solution:

For any $x_1 < x_2$ in $[a, b]$, MVT guarantees $c \in (x_1, x_2)$ such that:

$$f(x_2) - f(x_1) = f'(c)(x_2 - x_1)$$

Since $f'(c) > 0$ and $x_2 - x_1 > 0$, we have $f(x_2) - f(x_1) > 0 \implies f(x_1) < f(x_2)$.

Conclusion: $f$ is strictly increasing on $[a, b]$. $\blacksquare$
PROBLEM 8

Constant Derivative Implies Linearity

Suppose $f$ is differentiable on $(-\infty, \infty)$ and $f'(x) = m$ (constant). Prove $f$ is linear.

Solution:

For any $x \neq 0$, applying MVT on $[0, x]$ gives:

$$\frac{f(x) - f(0)}{x - 0} = f'(c) = m \implies f(x) = mx + f(0)$$
Conclusion: $f(x) = mx + C$ where $C = f(0)$. $\blacksquare$
PROBLEM 9

Optimization — Minimal Perimeter

Of all rectangles with area 1, which has the smallest perimeter?

Solution:

Let side lengths be $x$ and $y$ with $xy = 1 \implies y = 1/x$. Perimeter $P(x) = 2x + \frac{2}{x}$.

$P'(x) = 2 - \frac{2}{x^2} = 0 \implies x = 1$. $P''(1) = 4 > 0$ (minimum). Corresponding $y = 1$.

Conclusion: The square with dimensions $1 \times 1$ minimizes perimeter. $\blacksquare$
PROBLEM 10

Curve Sketching & Complete Function Analysis

Perform a complete calculus analysis for $f(x) = \frac{(2 + x)^2}{1 + x}$.

Solution & Analysis:

  • Domain: $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{-1\}$
  • Form: $f(x) = \frac{x^2 + 4x + 4}{x + 1} = x + 3 + \frac{1}{x + 1}$
  • Derivatives: $f'(x) = 1 - \frac{1}{(x+1)^2} = \frac{x(x+2)}{(x+1)^2}$, $f''(x) = \frac{2}{(x+1)^3}$
  • Critical Points: $x = 0 \implies (0, 4)$ (Local Min), $x = -2 \implies (-2, 0)$ (Local Max)
  • Asymptotes: Vertical asymptote $x = -1$; Slant asymptote $y = x + 3$
Conclusion: Local max at $(-2, 0)$, local min at $(0, 4)$, vertical asymptote $x = -1$, slant asymptote $y = x + 3$. $\blacksquare$
PROBLEM 11

Optimization — Maximizing Trapezoid Area

Three sides of a trapezoid each have length $L = 1$. What should be the length of the fourth side (the larger base) if the area of the trapezoid is to be as large as possible?

Step-by-Step Derivation & Geometry:

Method 1: Algebraic Base Optimization

Let the bottom base be $b = 1 + 2x$, where $x$ is the horizontal projection of each slanted side ($0 < x < 1$). The height is $h = \sqrt{1 - x^2}$.

The area of the trapezoid is:

$$A(x) = \frac{\text{top base} + \text{bottom base}}{2} \cdot h = \frac{1 + (1 + 2x)}{2} \sqrt{1 - x^2} = (1 + x)\sqrt{1 - x^2}$$

Differentiating $A(x)$ with respect to $x$ using the Product and Chain Rules:

$$A'(x) = 1 \cdot \sqrt{1 - x^2} + (1 + x) \cdot \frac{-2x}{2\sqrt{1 - x^2}} = \frac{(1 - x^2) - x(1 + x)}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}} = \frac{1 - x - 2x^2}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}$$

Setting $A'(x) = 0$ gives $2x^2 + x - 1 = 0 \implies (2x - 1)(x + 1) = 0$. Since $x > 0$, we select $x = \frac{1}{2}$.

The length of the bottom base is $b = 1 + 2\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = 2$.

Method 2: Trigonometric Optimization

Let $\theta$ be the base angle ($0 < \theta < \frac{\pi}{2}$). Then $h = \sin\theta$ and $x = \cos\theta$.

$$A(\theta) = (1 + \cos\theta)\sin\theta = \sin\theta + \frac{1}{2}\sin(2\theta)$$ $$A'(\theta) = \cos\theta + \cos(2\theta) = \cos\theta + 2\cos^2\theta - 1 = (2\cos\theta - 1)(\cos\theta + 1) = 0$$

For $\theta \in \left(0, \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$, $\cos\theta = \frac{1}{2} \implies \theta = \frac{\pi}{3} (60^\circ)$. Thus the bottom base is $1 + 2\cos(60^\circ) = 2$.

Conclusion: The length of the fourth side (larger base) must be 2 (yielding a regular semi-hexagon). $\blacksquare$
PROBLEM 12

Tangent Line Equation & Linear Approximation

Let $f(x) = \frac{2x}{x^2 + 3}$.
  1. Find the tangent line equation at $x = 1$.
  2. Estimate $f(1.2)$ using linear approximation.

Solution:

$f(1) = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$. Derivatives: $f'(x) = \frac{2(x^2+3) - 2x(2x)}{(x^2+3)^2} = \frac{6 - 2x^2}{(x^2+3)^2} \implies f'(1) = \frac{4}{16} = \frac{1}{4}$.

  • Tangent Line: $y - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{4}(x - 1) \implies y = \frac{1}{4}x + \frac{1}{4}$.
  • Linear Approximation: $f(1.2) \approx \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4}(0.2) = 0.5 + 0.05 = 0.55$.
Conclusion: Tangent line is $y = \frac{1}{4}x + \frac{1}{4}$; $f(1.2) \approx 0.55$. $\blacksquare$
PROBLEM 13

Limit Involving Derivatives and Conjugates

Suppose $f'(2) = 3$. Find the limit: $\lim_{x \to 2} \frac{f(x) - f(2)}{\sqrt{x} - \sqrt{2}}$.

Solution:

$$\lim_{x \to 2} \frac{f(x) - f(2)}{\sqrt{x} - \sqrt{2}} = \lim_{x \to 2} \left[ \frac{f(x) - f(2)}{x - 2} \cdot (\sqrt{x} + \sqrt{2}) \right] = f'(2) \cdot 2\sqrt{2} = 3 \cdot 2\sqrt{2} = 6\sqrt{2}$$
Conclusion: The limit value is $6\sqrt{2}$. $\blacksquare$
PROBLEM 14

Distance Optimization to a Parabola

Find the point on the parabola $y = x^2$ for $0 < x < 1$ nearest to $(0, 1)$.

Solution:

Square distance from $(x, x^2)$ to $(0, 1)$ is $D(x) = x^2 + (x^2 - 1)^2 = x^4 - x^2 + 1$.

$D'(x) = 4x^3 - 2x = 2x(2x^2 - 1) = 0 \implies x = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$. Corresponding $y = \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{2}$.

Conclusion: The nearest point is $\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \frac{1}{2}\right)$. $\blacksquare$
PROBLEM 15

Least Squares Optimization

Given numbers $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$, find $x$ minimizing $S(x) = \sum_{i=1}^n (x - a_i)^2$.

Solution:

$S'(x) = \sum_{i=1}^n 2(x - a_i) = 2nx - 2\sum_{i=1}^n a_i = 0 \implies x = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n a_i$.

Since $S''(x) = 2n > 0$, this yields a global minimum.

Conclusion: The optimal value is the arithmetic mean $x = \bar{a}$. $\blacksquare$
PROBLEM 16

Arc Length Derivation & Calculation

Find the arc length of $f(x) = \frac{2}{3}x^{3/2}$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 3$.

Solution:

$f'(x) = \sqrt{x} \implies \sqrt{1 + [f'(x)]^2} = \sqrt{1 + x}$.

$$L = \int_0^3 \sqrt{1 + x} \, dx = \left[ \frac{2}{3}(1 + x)^{3/2} \right]_0^3 = \frac{2}{3}(8 - 1) = \frac{14}{3}$$
Conclusion: Arc length is $\frac{14}{3}$ units. $\blacksquare$
PROBLEM 17

Total Arc Length of an Astroid

Use symmetry to find the total arc length of the astroid curve given by $x^{2/3} + y^{2/3} = 1$.

Detailed Geometric Explanation & Visualization:

Step-by-Step Integration:

1. In Quadrant 1 ($0 \le x \le 1$), explicit form is $y = (1 - x^{2/3})^{3/2}$.

2. Differentiating with respect to $x$:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{3}{2}(1 - x^{2/3})^{1/2} \cdot \left(-\frac{2}{3}x^{-1/3}\right) = -\frac{(1 - x^{2/3})^{1/2}}{x^{1/3}}$$

3. Arc length integrand element:

$$1 + \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2 = 1 + \frac{1 - x^{2/3}}{x^{2/3}} = \frac{x^{2/3} + 1 - x^{2/3}}{x^{2/3}} = \frac{1}{x^{2/3}} = x^{-2/3}$$ $$\sqrt{1 + (y')^2} = x^{-1/3}$$

4. Integrating over Quadrant I:

$$L_1 = \int_0^1 x^{-1/3} \, dx = \left[ \frac{3}{2} x^{2/3} \right]_0^1 = \frac{3}{2}$$

5. Multiplying by 4 for total symmetric length: $L = 4 \times \frac{3}{2} = 6$.

Conclusion: Total arc length of the astroid is 6. $\blacksquare$
PROBLEM 18

Optimization — Maximizing Inscribed Triangle Area

The line $y = mx + b$ intersects the parabola $y = x^2$ at points $A$ and $B$. Find the point $P$ on the parabolic arc $AOB$ maximizing the area of triangle $PAB$.

Geometric Principle & Visualization:

Geometric Proof:

The base length $AB$ is fixed. The area of triangle $PAB$ is $\frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$. Thus, maximum area occurs when the perpendicular distance (height) from $P$ to the secant line $y = mx + b$ is maximized.

The point of maximum perpendicular distance on a smooth curve to a secant line occurs precisely where the tangent line to the curve is parallel to the secant line.

Secant line slope $= m$. Tangent line slope $= \frac{d}{dx}(x^2) = 2x$.

$$2x = m \implies x = \frac{m}{2}$$

Substituting into the parabola $y = x^2$ yields $y = \left(\frac{m}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{m^2}{4}$.

Conclusion: The optimal point is $P = \left(\frac{m}{2}, \frac{m^2}{4}\right)$. $\blacksquare$
PROBLEM 19

Symmetry Properties of Integrals

Let $s : [-a, a] \to \mathbb{R}$ be integrable. Prove:
  1. If $s$ is even ($s(-t) = s(t)$), then $\int_{-a}^a s(t) \, dt = 2\int_0^a s(t) \, dt$.
  2. If $s$ is odd ($s(-t) = -s(t)$), then $\int_{-a}^a s(t) \, dt = 0$.

Proof:

Split: $\int_{-a}^a s(t)dt = \int_{-a}^0 s(t)dt + \int_0^a s(t)dt$. Using $u = -t$ on the first integral yields $\int_0^a s(-u)du$.

  • Even: $\int_0^a s(u)du + \int_0^a s(t)dt = 2\int_0^a s(t)dt$.
  • Odd: $-\int_0^a s(u)du + \int_0^a s(t)dt = 0$. $\blacksquare$

PROBLEM 21

Integrals of Step/Greatest Integer Functions

Prove for $n \in \mathbb{N}$:
  1. $\int_0^n \lfloor x \rfloor \, dx = \frac{n(n - 1)}{2}$
  2. $\int_0^{n^2} \lfloor \sqrt{x} \rfloor \, dx = \frac{n(n - 1)(4n + 1)}{6}$

Proof:

a) $\int_0^n \lfloor x \rfloor dx = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \int_k^{k+1} k \, dx = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} k = \frac{n(n-1)}{2}$.

b) $\int_0^{n^2} \lfloor \sqrt{x} \rfloor dx = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \int_{k^2}^{(k+1)^2} k \, dx = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} k(2k + 1) = 2\sum k^2 + \sum k = \frac{n(n-1)(4n+1)}{6}$. $\blacksquare$

PROBLEM 25

PROBLEM 26

Rolle's Theorem & Real Roots of Polynomial Derivatives

Let $p(x)$ be a polynomial of degree $n$ with $n$ real roots. Show that all roots of $p'(x)$ are real.

Proof:

Let $p(x)$ have $k$ distinct real roots with multiplicities $e_1, \dots, e_k$ where $\sum e_i = n$.

  1. Multiplicities contribute $\sum(e_i - 1) = n - k$ real roots for $p'$.
  2. Rolle's Theorem between adjacent distinct roots gives $k - 1$ real roots for $p'$.

Total real roots = $(n - k) + (k - 1) = n - 1 = \deg(p')$. $\blacksquare$

Closure of the Unit Interval under Multiplication

Theorem: If $a, b \in [0,1]$, then their product satisfies $a \cdot b \in [0,1]$.

Part 1: Squaring Lemma

If $a=0 \implies a^2=0 \in [0,1]$. If $a=1 \implies a^2=1 \in [0,1]$. If $0 < a < 1 \implies 0 < a^2 < a < 1$. Thus $a^2 \in [0,1]$.

Part 2: Exhaustive Case Analysis

If $a=0$ or $b=0$, $ab=0 \in [0,1]$. If $a=1$, $ab=b \in [0,1]$. If $0 < a < b < 1$, $0 < ab < b < 1 \implies ab \in (0,1) \subset [0,1]$. $\blacksquare$

Discontinuity of the Dirichlet Function

Show that the Dirichlet function $D(x) = 1$ ($x \in \mathbb{Q}$) and $0$ ($x \notin \mathbb{Q}$) is discontinuous at every point $a \in \mathbb{R}$.

Proof:

Set $\varepsilon = 1/2$. By density of rationals and irrationals, every interval $(a - \delta, a + \delta)$ contains points where $D(x)$ takes values $0$ and $1$. Hence $|D(x) - D(a)| = 1 \ge 1/2 = \varepsilon$, so $D$ is discontinuous everywhere. $\blacksquare$

QUIZ 1 — Q3

Continuity of $f(x) = x^2$ via $\varepsilon$–$\delta$ Definition

Prove using $\varepsilon$–$\delta$ that $\lim_{x \to a} x^2 = a^2$ for any $a \in \mathbb{R}$.

Proof:

|x^2 - a^2| = |x - a||x + a|. Constrain $\delta \le 1 \implies |x + a| < 1 + 2|a|$. Choose $\delta = \min\left\{1, \frac{\varepsilon}{1 + 2|a|}\right\}$. Then $|x^2 - a^2| < \left(\frac{\varepsilon}{1 + 2|a|}\right)(1 + 2|a|) = \varepsilon$. $\blacksquare$

QUIZ 1 — Q4

Existence of $n$th Roots via Intermediate Value Theorem

Prove that given $a \ge 0$ and $n \in \mathbb{N}$, there exists a unique real number $c \ge 0$ such that $c^n = a$.

Proof:

For $a > 0$, define $g(x) = x^n - a$ on $[0, M]$ where $M = \max\{1, a\}$. $g(0) = -a < 0$ and $g(M) \ge 0$. By IVT, $g(c) = 0 \implies c^n = a$. Uniqueness follows from strict monotonicity of $x^n$. $\blacksquare$

Bounded Functions and Riemann Integrability

a) Is every bounded function integrable? b) Is every bounded, increasing function integrable?

Solution:

Rolle's Theorem Evaluation

Verify Rolle's theorem for $f(x) = x^3 - 6x^2 + 11x - 6$ on $[1, 3]$ and find all valid $c \in (1, 3)$.

Solution:

$f(1) = f(3) = 0$. $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 12x + 11 = 0 \implies x = 2 \pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$.

Values: $c_1 \approx 1.423$ and $c_2 \approx 2.577$ (both in $(1, 3)$). $\blacksquare$
QUIZ 2 — Q2

Lagrange's Mean Value Theorem Inequality

Prove $\frac{x}{1+x} < \ln(1+x) < x$ for $x > 0$ and find $c$ for $x = 0.2$.

Solution:

MVT on $[0, x]$ for $f(t) = \ln(1+t) \implies \frac{\ln(1+x)}{x} = \frac{1}{1+c}$. Since $0 < c < x \implies \frac{1}{1+x} < \frac{1}{1+c} < 1$.

For $x = 0.2$: $\frac{\ln(1.2)}{0.2} = \frac{1}{1+c} \implies c \approx 0.0970$. $\blacksquare$

QUIZ 2 — Q3

Optimization — Box with Square Base

An open rectangular box with square base has volume $32 \text{ m}^3$. Base costs ₹3/m$^2$, sides ₹2/m$^2$. Find optimal dimensions and minimum cost.

Solution:

$h = \frac{32}{x^2}$. Cost $C(x) = 3x^2 + 8xh = 3x^2 + \frac{256}{x}$.

$C'(x) = 6x - \frac{256}{x^2} = 0 \implies x = (128/3)^{1/3} \approx 3.49 \text{ m}$, $h \approx 2.62 \text{ m}$. Minimum cost $\approx ₹109.89$. $\blacksquare$

QUIZ 2 — Q4

Curve Tracing: $y = \frac{x^2 - 1}{x^2 - 4}$

Perform complete curve analysis for $y = 1 + \frac{3}{x^2 - 4}$.

Solution:

Domain $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{\pm 2\}$, even symmetry. Intercepts $(\pm 1, 0)$ and $(0, 0.25)$. Vertical asymptotes $x = \pm 2$, horizontal asymptote $y = 1$. Local max at $(0, 0.25)$. Concave up on $|x| > 2$, concave down on $|x| < 2$. $\blacksquare$

QUIZ 2 — Q5

Taylor Polynomial for $\sqrt{1+x}$

Find degree 3 Taylor polynomial $P_3(x)$ for $f(x) = \sqrt{1+x}$ about $x = 0$ and estimate $\sqrt{1.2}$.

Solution:

$P_3(x) = 1 + \frac{x}{2} - \frac{x^2}{8} + \frac{x^3}{16}$.

$P_3(0.2) = 1 + 0.1 - 0.005 + 0.0005 = 1.0955$. Actual $\sqrt{1.2} \approx 1.095445$ (Error $\approx 5.49 \times 10^{-5}$). $\blacksquare$

QUIZ 3 — Q1

Riemann Integrability of Infinite Spike Function

Define $f: [0,1] \to \mathbb{R}$ by: $$f(x) = \begin{cases} 1, & x = \frac{1}{n} \text{ for some } n \in \{1, 2, 3, \dots\} \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$ (a) Show directly from the definition (using upper and lower Darboux sums) that $f$ is Riemann integrable on $[0,1]$.
(b) Compute $\displaystyle\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx$.

Step 1 — Lower Sum Evaluation and Strategy

On every subinterval $[x_{i-1}, x_i] \subseteq [0,1]$, there exist irrational numbers and non-reciprocal rationals where $f(x) = 0$. Thus, the infimum (glb) on any subinterval is always $m_i = \inf_{x \in [x_{i-1}, x_i]} f(x) = 0$. Consequently, for any partition $P$, the lower Darboux sum is: \[ L(f,P) = \sum_{i=1}^k m_i \Delta x_i = 0 \implies \underline{\int_0^1} f(x)\,dx = 0 \] Therefore, to establish Riemann integrability via Darboux's criterion ($U(f,P) - L(f,P) < \varepsilon$), it is sufficient to show that we can construct a partition $P$ such that $U(f,P) < \varepsilon$ for any arbitrary $\varepsilon > 0$.

Step 2 — Partition Construction

Given any challenge threshold $\varepsilon > 0$:

  1. Choose an integer $N$ sufficiently large such that $\frac{1}{N} < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}$.
  2. On the interval $[\frac{1}{N}, 1]$, the points where $f(x) = 1$ are strictly finite: $S = \{1, \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{3}, \dots, \frac{1}{N}\}$.
  3. Around each point $y_k = \frac{1}{k} \in S$, construct a tiny subinterval of width $2\delta_k$ such that the total sum of widths of all $N$ boxes is less than $\frac{\varepsilon}{2}$.
Spikes at $x = 1, \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{3}, \dots$; pink strips enclose each spike with arbitrarily small total length.

Step 3 — Bounding Upper Sum $U(f,P)$

We partition $[0,1]$ and evaluate the upper sum $U(f,P) = \sum M_i \Delta x_i$ by grouping the subintervals:

  • Tail interval $[0, \frac{1}{N}]$: Here $M_1 = 1$, and its width is $\Delta x_1 = \frac{1}{N} < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}$. Its contribution to $U(f,P)$ is $< \frac{\varepsilon}{2}$.
  • The $N$ tiny spike boxes: On each box, $M_i = 1$. The sum of widths of all $N$ boxes is chosen to be $< \frac{\varepsilon}{2}$. Their contribution is $< \frac{\varepsilon}{2}$.
  • Remaining gap subintervals: $f(x) \equiv 0$, so $M_i = 0$ with zero contribution.

Summing these up gives $U(f,P) < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon$. Since $L(f,P) = 0$, $U(f,P) - L(f,P) < \varepsilon$. Thus, $f$ is Riemann integrable on $[0,1]$.

Step 4 — Value of the Integral (Part b)

Since both the lower integral ($\underline{\int_0^1} f(x)\,dx = 0$) and upper integral ($\overline{\int_0^1} f(x)\,dx = 0$) coincide:

Conclusion: $\displaystyle\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx = 0$. $\blacksquare$
QUIZ 3 — Q2

Area Between Two Curves

Find area between $y = x^3$ and $y = 3x^2 - 2x$ for $x \in [0, 2]$.

Solution:

Intersections at $x = 0, 1, 2$. Area $= \int_0^1 (x^3 - 3x^2 + 2x)dx + \int_1^2 (-x^3 + 3x^2 - 2x)dx = \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$. $\blacksquare$

QUIZ 3 — Q3

Arc Length, Volume and Surface of $y = x^2$

For $y = x^2$ on $[0, 1]$, compute: a) Arc length, b) Volume of revolution about $x$-axis, c) Surface area.

Solution:

  • Arc Length: $L = \int_0^1 \sqrt{1+4x^2} dx = \frac{2\sqrt{5} + \ln(2+\sqrt{5})}{4} \approx 1.4789$.
  • Volume: $V = \pi \int_0^1 x^4 dx = \frac{\pi}{5} \approx 0.6283$.
  • Surface Area: $S = 2\pi \int_0^1 x^2\sqrt{1+4x^2} dx = \frac{\pi}{32}[18\sqrt{5} - \ln(2+\sqrt{5})] \approx 3.8098$. $\blacksquare$
QUIZ 3 — Q4

Fundamental Theorem of Calculus in Action

Let $f(t) = 1$ on $[0, 1]$ and $2$ on $(1, 2]$. Find $F(x) = \int_0^x f(t) dt$.

Solution:

$F(x) = x$ for $0 \le x \le 1$, and $2x - 1$ for $1 < x \le 2$. $F(x)$ is continuous everywhere on $[0, 2]$, but fails to be differentiable at $x = 1$ due to jump in $f(t)$. $\blacksquare$

QUIZ 3 — Q5

Quadratic Polynomial from Integral Conditions

Find $P(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$ such that $P(0) = P(1) = 0$ and $\int_0^1 P(x) dx = 1$.

Solution:

$P(0) = 0 \implies c = 0$. $P(1) = 0 \implies b = -a \implies P(x) = a(x^2 - x)$.

$\int_0^1 a(x^2 - x) dx = a(1/3 - 1/2) = -a/6 = 1 \implies a = -6$.

Result: $P(x) = 6x(1 - x) = -6x^2 + 6x$. $\blacksquare$
