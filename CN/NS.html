Notes

  • ℕ = {1, 2, 3, …}
  • ℤ = {…, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, …}
  • ℚ = {p⁄q : p,q∈ℤ, q≠0}
  • 𝕀 = {x∈ℝ : x∉ℚ}
  • ℝ = ℚ ∪ 𝕀
  • Group means Closure, Associative, Existence of Identity, Existence of inverse

Counterexamples

  • Identity, ℕ, +: need e with a+e=a ∀a, forcing e=0, but 0∉ℕ.
  • Inverse, ℕ, +: 1+b=0 has no solution b∈ℕ.
  • Closure, 𝕀, +: √2+(−√2)=0∉𝕀.
  • Closure, 𝕀, ×: √2×√2=2∉𝕀.
  • Identity, 𝕀: 0∉𝕀 and 1∉𝕀 — neither identity exists.
  • Inverse, ℕ & ℤ, ×: 2×b=1 has no solution b∈ℕ or ℤ.

Key Takeaways

  • ℕ, ℤ, ℚ, ℝ are closed under +, ×. 𝕀 is closed under neither.
  • Associativity & commutativity hold everywhere (inherited from ℝ) — they don't need closure.
  • ℕ has no additive identity (0∉ℕ), so no additive inverses either.
  • 𝕀 has no identity for + or × (0∉𝕀, 1∉𝕀), so "inverse" isn't meaningful for 𝕀 alone.
  • Excluding 0, ℚ\{0} and ℝ\{0} are multiplicative groups; ℤ and ℕ are not (e.g. 2 has no integer inverse).
