What is a Bijective Map?

A map $f: A \to B$ is bijective (one-to-one and onto) if every input in $A$ targets a unique output in $B$, covering all of $B$ perfectly without overlaps or gaps.

Rubber Band Physical Model: Think of $[0,1]$ as an elastic band. Mapping it to $[0,2]$ stretches it, mapping it to $[1,2]$ shifts (translates) it without stretching, and combining both yields general interval transformations $[a,b] \to [c,d]$.
1

Bijective map $f:[0,1] \to [0,2]$

How do we double the length of the interval $[0,1]$ to cover $[0,2]$ completely?

Solution: $f(x) = 2x$

Rubber Band Analogy: Pin $x = 0$ at $0$, and pull $x = 1$ to $2$. Every point $x \in [0,1]$ stretches to twice its original position.

  • Injective (one-to-one): $2x_1 = 2x_2 \implies x_1 = x_2$
  • Surjective (onto): For any $y \in [0,2]$, $x = y/2 \in [0,1]$

Pure Translation: Bijective Map $f:[0,1] \to [1,2]$

Notice that interval $[0,1]$ and interval $[1,2]$ have the exact same length ($1 - 0 = 1$ and $2 - 1 = 1$). No stretching is required! We simply slide or translate the entire interval to the right by $1$ unit.

Translation Map Equation:

$f(x) = x + 1$

• Start endpoint: $f(0) = 0 + 1 = 1$
• End endpoint: $f(1) = 1 + 1 = 2$

Key Concept: Adding a constant $c$ shifts the interval rigidly without changing its size.

Combining Both: Map $f:[0,1] \to [4,9]$

Now we combine Stretching and Translation using function composition!

1. Stretch (Length = 5)

Stretch $[0,1]$ to $[0,5]$

$f(x) = 5x$
2. Translate (Shift +4)

Shift $[0,5]$ to $[4,9]$

$g(u) = u + 4$
Composition $(g \circ f)$

Combine step 1 & 2

$(g \circ f)(x) = 5x + 4$

Composition Mindmap Diagram

Step A: Normalize $[a,b] \to [0,1]$
$f(x) = \frac{x-a}{b-a}$
Step B: Scale & Shift $[0,1] \to [c,d]$
$g(t) = (d-c)t + c$
Composition $(g \circ f): [a,b] \to [c,d]$
$(g \circ f)(x) = \left(\frac{x-a}{b-a}\right)(d-c) + c$

Inverse Task: $f(x) = x + 1$ and $g(x) = x - 1$

Show that for point $(a, f(a))$, the point $(f(a), y)$ lies on $g(x)$ when $y = a$

Algebraic Inverse Proof

Let $f(x) = x + 1$ and $g(x) = x - 1$.

• $(g \circ f)(a) = g(f(a)) = g(a+1) = (a+1) - 1 = a$

• $(f \circ g)(a) = f(g(a)) = f(a-1) = (a-1) + 1 = a$

For point $(a, f(a))$, substituting $x = f(a)$ into $g(x)$ gives $y = g(f(a)) = a$. Hence, $(f(a), a)$ lies on the graph of $g(x)$.

Direct Geometric Method using $\mathbb{R}^2$ Coordinates

Simplifying interval mappings using straight line equations on the Cartesian plane

A Simpler Perspective: Instead of mapping endpoints via step-by-step transformations or bridge intervals, there is a simple and elegant way to achieve this using 2D Coordinate Geometry in $\mathbb{R}^2$.

Cartesian Diagram in $\mathbb{R}^2$

Suppose we want $f:[a,b] \to [c,d]$ to be a bijection with $f(a) = c$ and $f(b) = d$.

This requirement simply means that the graph of $f$ is a straight line segment connecting the points (a, c) and (b, d) on the $\mathbb{R}^2$ Cartesian plane.

1. Two-Point Line Equation:
$$y - c = \left(\frac{d - c}{b - a}\right)(x - a)$$
2. Direct Explicit Function:
$$f(x) = \left(\frac{x - a}{b - a}\right)(d - c) + c$$
