Why does $\dfrac{\sin x}{x} \to 1$ as $x \to 0$?

A geometric proof by the Squeeze Theorem — drag the slider, watch the picture prove the inequality.

INTERACTIVE PROOF

1The Setup

Consider the unit circle centered at the origin $O$.

  • Let $A=(1,0)$ be a fixed point on the circle.
  • Let $x$ be a small angle (in radians), and let $B=(\cos x,\sin x)$ be the point on the circle with $\angle AOB = x$.
  • Draw the tangent line to the circle at $A$ (a vertical line, since $OA$ is horizontal).
  • Extend the ray $OB$ until it meets that tangent line at a point $T$.

We will compare the areas of three regions that all sit on top of one another: a small triangle, a sector, and a big triangle.

3Turning Areas into an Inequality

The three areas are:

$$\text{Area}_{\triangle OAB}=\tfrac12\sin x,\qquad \text{Area}_{\text{sector}}=\tfrac12 x,\qquad \text{Area}_{\triangle OAT}=\tfrac12\tan x \quad (x>0)$$

Since the triangle sits inside the sector, which sits inside the bigger triangle:

$$\tfrac12\sin x \;\le\; \tfrac12 x \;\le\; \tfrac12\tan x \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sin x \le x \le \tan x$$

Dividing through by $\sin x>0$ (valid for $0<x<\pi/2$):

$$1 \le \frac{x}{\sin x} \le \frac{1}{\cos x} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \cos x \le \frac{\sin x}{x} \le 1$$

What happens when $x<0$?
Let $x=-y$ with $y>0$. Then $\cos(x)=\cos(-y)=\cos(y)$, so cosine is unchanged, and $$\frac{\sin x}{x}=\frac{\sin(-y)}{-y}=\frac{-\sin y}{-y}=\frac{\sin y}{y}>0.$$ So for $-\tfrac{\pi}{2}<x<\tfrac{\pi}{2}$, both $\cos x>0$ and $\dfrac{\sin x}{x}>0$, and the same squeeze $$\cos x \le \frac{\sin x}{x} \le 1$$ holds on both sides of $0$. Drag the slider in section 2 to negative angles — the picture (with $B$ and $T$ reflected below the axis) still shows $|\sin x|\le |x|\le|\tan x|$.

4Squeezing to the Limit

As $x\to 0$, the left fence $\cos x \to 1$ and the right fence is already $1$. The curve $\sin x/x$ is trapped between them, so it has no choice but to be squeezed to $1$ as well.

y = sin(x)/x y = cos(x) y = 1 (the limit)

At x = 0.5585 rad: cos x = 0.8479, sin x/x = 0.9497
