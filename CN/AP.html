Preliminary

Least Upper Bound Property

Every nonempty subset $S$ of $\mathbb{R}$ that is bounded above has a least upper bound in $\mathbb{R}$. That is, there exists $\mathrm{lub}(S)\in\mathbb{R}$ such that:

  1. $\mathrm{lub}(S)$ is an upper bound of $S$: for all $s\in S$, $s \leq \mathrm{lub}(S)$.
  2. $\mathrm{lub}(S)$ is the least such bound: if $b$ is any upper bound of $S$, then $\mathrm{lub}(S)\leq b$.
AP1 — Archimedean Property

Natural Numbers Are Unbounded

The set $\mathbb{N}$ of natural numbers is not bounded above in $\mathbb{R}$.

Proof — by contradiction, using the LUB Property

1

Suppose, for contradiction, that $\mathbb{N}$ is bounded above in $\mathbb{R}$.

2

Since $\mathbb{N}$ is nonempty and bounded above, the LUB Property guarantees a least upper bound. Let $\alpha = \mathrm{lub}(\mathbb{N})$.

3

Because $\alpha$ is an upper bound, $n \leq \alpha$ for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$.

4

Since $\alpha$ is the least upper bound, $\alpha - 1$ is not an upper bound of $\mathbb{N}$. Hence there exists $n_0 \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $\alpha - 1 < n_0$.

!

Contradiction. We have $n_0 + 1 \in \mathbb{N}$ and $n_0 + 1 > \alpha$, which contradicts that $\alpha$ is an upper bound of $\mathbb{N}$.

✓

Therefore, $\mathbb{N}$ is not bounded above in $\mathbb{R}$.

AP2 — Archimedean Property (Multiplicative Form)

Multiples Surpass Any Bound

If $x > 0$ and $y \in \mathbb{R}$, then there exists $n \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $nx > y$.

Geometric Interpretation

On the number line, $x > 0$ is a small length near the origin. The point $y$ lies far to the right.

Mark $x$ on the line.

Add $x$ to itself: $x + x = 2x$.

$2x + x = 3x$.

$3x + x = 4x$.

$4x + x = 5x$.

$5x + x = 6x$.

$6x + x = 7x$ — still short of $y$.

$7x + x = 8x$. Now $8x > y$! We have found $n = 8$ with $nx > y$.

A Fable

The Tortoise and the Rabbit

Rabbit sprints ahead, sleeps just before the target $y$, thinking the tortoise — with its tiny step $x$ — can never cross $y$. But the tortoise knows the Archimedean Property.

Rabbit dashes to just before $y$ and falls asleep, confident the slow tortoise can never reach here.

Tortoise takes one step: reaches $x$.

Another step: $2x$. Rabbit snores on.

$3x$. Slow, but steady.

$4x$.

$5x$. Getting closer.

$6x$.

$7x$ — right behind the sleeping rabbit!

$8x > y$! Tortoise crosses the rabbit. Slow and steady wins — the Archimedean Property.

A Thought Experiment

Walking to the Moon

If a man does not die, then he may reach the Moon by walking. Each step is small — but the Archimedean Property says it is enough.

The Moon is about 384,400 km away. A man's step is roughly 0.7 m — infinitesimally small by comparison.

One step. Distance covered: $x$.

Two steps, three steps. $2x$, $3x$.

$4x$, $5x$, $6x$\ldots the distance grows, but the Moon seems untouched.

$7x$, $8x$, $9x$\ldots the steps keep adding.

$10x$, $11x$, $12x$\ldots

By AP2: for any distance $y$ and step size $x > 0$, there exists $n \in \mathbb{N}$ with $nx > y$.

If the man does not die, the Archimedean Property guarantees he reaches the Moon by walking.

Proof of AP2 — using AP1

1

Let $x > 0$ and $y \in \mathbb{R}$. Since $x \neq 0$, the quotient $\dfrac{y}{x}$ is a well-defined real number.

2

By AP1, $\mathbb{N}$ is not bounded above, so $\dfrac{y}{x}$ cannot be an upper bound of $\mathbb{N}$.

3

Hence there exists $n \in \mathbb{N}$ with $n > \dfrac{y}{x}$. Multiplying by $x > 0$ gives $nx > y$.

Equivalence

Proving AP1 Using AP2

Proof — AP1 follows from AP2

1

Let $M \in \mathbb{R}$ be arbitrary. We show that $M$ is not an upper bound of $\mathbb{N}$.

2

Apply AP2 with $x = 1 > 0$ and $y = M$. There exists $n \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $n \cdot 1 > M$, i.e., $n > M$.

3

Since $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and $n > M$, the number $M$ fails to be an upper bound of $\mathbb{N}$.

✓

As $M$ was arbitrary, no real number can be an upper bound of $\mathbb{N}$. Therefore $\mathbb{N}$ is not bounded above.

Corollary

Reciprocals Become Arbitrarily Small

For every $x > 0$, there exists $n \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $\dfrac{1}{n} < x$.

Proof

1

Let $x > 0$ be given. Apply AP2 with $y = 1$ and this $x > 0$.

2

There exists $n \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $nx > 1$.

✓

Since $n > 0$, dividing both sides by $n$ yields $x > \dfrac{1}{n}$, i.e., $\dfrac{1}{n} < x$.

In words: no matter how small a positive number $x$ you choose, some reciprocal $\frac{1}{n}$ is even smaller. The sequence $\frac{1}{1},\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{3},\ldots$ squeezes toward zero.
