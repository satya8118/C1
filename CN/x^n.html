Mathematical Monotonicity Proof

Proving $b^n > a^n$ via MVT

A rigorous step-by-step calculus journey demonstrating how the Mean Value Theorem bridges derivative positivity with algebraic monotonicity.

📜

1. The Theorem

Statement

Let $a, b \in \mathbb{R}$ and let $n \in \mathbb{N}$ with $n \ge 1$.

$$\text{If } b > a > 0, \quad \text{then } b^n > a^n$$

*Note: We assume strict positivity for $a$ and $b$ to guarantee strict ordering for all $n \ge 1$.

🛠️

2. The Tool: MVT

If a function $f: [a,b] \to \mathbb{R}$ meets two requirements:

  • 1 Continuous on closed interval $[a, b]$
  • 2 Differentiable on open interval $(a, b)$

MVT Core Equation

There exists at least one $c \in (a, b)$ such that:

$$f(b) - f(a) = f'(c)(b - a)$$
💡

Key Takeaways

Bridge Calculus & Algebra

Differential tools (derivatives & continuous slopes) cleanly solve fundamental algebraic inequalities.

The Role of Point $c$

The intermediate point $c \in (a,b)$ acts as the critical conduit transferring derivative positivity into value growth.

Global Monotonicity

Because $f'(x) = n x^{n-1} > 0$ for all $x > 0$, the monomial function $f(x) = x^n$ is strictly increasing on $(0, \infty)$.

🚀

Step-by-Step Proof

5 Interactive Steps
1

Define the Function

Let $f(x) = x^n$. Because $n \ge 1$ is a natural number, $f(x)$ is a monomial polynomial.

Polynomials are continuous & differentiable everywhere on $\mathbb{R}$, fully satisfying MVT requirements.
2

Apply the Mean Value Theorem

Given $b > a > 0$, the interval $[a, b]$ is well-defined. Applying MVT guarantees a point $c \in (a, b)$ satisfying:

$$f(b) - f(a) = f'(c)(b - a)$$
3

Differentiate & Substitute

Applying the Power Rule yields $f'(x) = n x^{n-1}$. Evaluating at $x = c$ and substituting back:

$$b^n - a^n = n c^{n-1} (b - a)$$
4

Analyze Factor Signs (The "Aha!" Moment)

Examine each element on the right-hand side of $n \cdot c^{n-1} \cdot (b - a)$:

Factor Condition Sign Reason
$n$ $n \in \mathbb{N}, n \ge 1$ $+$ Positive natural integer
$c^{n-1}$ $c \in (a, b), a > 0 \implies c > 0$ $+$ Positive base raised to power
$(b - a)$ $b > a$ $+$ Greater minus lesser quantity
5

Final Conclusion

The product of three strictly positive real numbers is strictly positive:

$$n \cdot c^{n-1} \cdot (b - a) > 0 \implies b^n - a^n > 0$$
$$b^n > a^n \quad \blacksquare$$
