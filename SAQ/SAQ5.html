⚔️ High School Intuition vs. University Rigor

Concept High School View (Intuitive) University View ($\epsilon$-$\delta$ Rigor)
Continuity Definition "You can draw the graph without lifting your pencil." For every $\epsilon > 0$, $\exists \delta > 0$ such that $|x-c| < \delta \implies |f(x)-f(c)| < \epsilon$.
Discrete Domains ($\mathbb{N}$ or $\{1,2,3\}$) "Not continuous because it consists of separate floating dots." EVERYWHERE CONTINUOUS! Shrinking $\delta < 1$ isolates $x=c$, making condition vacuously true.
Role of $\epsilon$ & $\delta$ "Plug in numbers close to $c$ and see if answers are close." $\epsilon$ is an external challenge. $\delta$ is your defensive buffer size. $\delta$ depends on $\epsilon$ and point $c$.
Jump Discontinuities "Left limit doesn't equal right limit." If $\epsilon < \text{jump height}$, no single $\delta > 0$ can keep all values inside the $\epsilon$ target band.

🎯 The $\epsilon$-$\delta$ Game Framework

Think of $\epsilon$-$\delta$ proofs as a 2-player adversarial game:

  • Player 1 (Challenger): Picks an arbitrarily small vertical tolerance $\epsilon > 0$. They demand: "Keep $f(x)$ within $\epsilon$ of $f(c)$!"
  • Player 2 (Defender / You): Must respond with a horizontal tolerance $\delta > 0$ buffer around $c$.
  • Winning Rule: If you can ALWAYS produce a valid $\delta > 0$ no matter how tiny $\epsilon$ is, the function is continuous at $c$!

⚡ 6 Key Function Cheat Sheet

Constant $f(x)=k$: Any $\delta > 0$ works for all $\epsilon$.
Identity $f(x)=x$: Choose $\delta = \epsilon$.
Linear $f(x)=mx+b$: Choose $\delta = \epsilon / |m|$.
Floor $\lfloor x \rfloor$: At non-integers, $\delta = \min(c-\lfloor c\rfloor, \lceil c\rceil-c)$.
Discrete $\mathbb{N}$: Pick $\delta = 0.5$ (isolates point $c$).

💡 4 Study Tips for First-Year Proof Writing

1. Work Backwards on Scratch Paper Start with $|f(x) - L| < \epsilon$, factor and simplify until you isolate $|x - c| < \text{expression}$. That expression is your candidate $\delta$!
2. Always Start Proofs Formally Begin every writeup with: "Let $\epsilon > 0$ be given. Choose $\delta = \dots$" Never start with the conclusion!
3. Don't Rely Solely on Graphs Visual graphs are wonderful for building intuition, but a mathematical proof requires exact algebraic inequalities.
4. Remember Domain Restrictions Continuity is defined strictly on points $x \in \text{Domain}(f)$. Out-of-domain points do not count against continuity!
