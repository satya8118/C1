Calculus - Short Answer Practice Questions

Question 1
Create a function \( g: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R} \) which is continuous only at \( x = 0 \) and \( x = 1 \).
Hint: Modify Dirichlet's function using a continuous polynomial multiplier \( p(x) \) whose real roots are precisely at \( x = 0 \) and \( x = 1 \).
Solution:

Define \( g(x) \) as follows:

\[ g(x) = \begin{cases} x(x - 1), & \text{if } x \in \mathbb{Q} \\ 0, & \text{if } x \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \mathbb{Q} \end{cases} \]

Continuity Check:

  • At \( x \in \{0, 1\} \), \(\lim_{x \to 0} x(x-1) = 0\) and \(\lim_{x \to 0} 0 = 0\). By the Squeeze Theorem, \( g \) is continuous at \( x = 0 \) and \( x = 1 \).
  • At any point \( x_0 \notin \{0, 1\} \), the limit along rational points is \( x_0(x_0 - 1) \neq 0 \), while the limit along irrational points is \( 0 \). Since the limits along different sequences differ, \( g \) is discontinuous everywhere else.
Question 2
Suppose \( g \) is an even function and let \( h = f \circ g \). Is \( h \) always an even function? Justify your answer.
Hint: Evaluate \( h(-x) \) directly using the definition of composite functions and the given symmetry property \( g(-x) = g(x) \).
Solution:

Yes, \( h \) is always an even function.

Proof: By definition, \( g \) is an even function, which means \( g(-x) = g(x) \) for all \( x \in \mathbb{R} \).

Now evaluate \( h(-x) \):

\[ h(-x) = (f \circ g)(-x) = f(g(-x)) = f(g(x)) = h(x) \]

Since \( h(-x) = h(x) \) for all \( x \in \mathbb{R} \), \( h \) is always an even function, regardless of whether \( f \) is even, odd, or neither.

Question 3
Suppose \( g \) is an odd function and let \( h = f \circ g \). Is \( h \) always an odd function? What if \( f \) is odd? What if \( f \) is even? Justify your answer.
Hint: Express \( h(-x) = f(g(-x)) = f(-g(x)) \). Consider what happens when \( f \) is odd, when \( f \) is even, and when \( f \) has no specific symmetry.
Solution:

1. Is \( h \) ALWAYS an odd function?
No. In general, \( h \) is not guaranteed to be odd unless \( f \) itself satisfies a specific symmetry.

2. What if \( f \) is ODD?
If \( f \) is odd, then \( f(-u) = -f(u) \). Evaluating \( h(-x) \):

\[ h(-x) = f(g(-x)) = f(-g(x)) = -f(g(x)) = -h(x) \]

Thus, if both \( f \) and \( g \) are odd, \( h = f \circ g \) is ALWAYS an ODD function.

3. What if \( f \) is EVEN?
If \( f \) is even, then \( f(-u) = f(u) \). Evaluating \( h(-x) \):

\[ h(-x) = f(g(-x)) = f(-g(x)) = f(g(x)) = h(x) \]

Thus, if \( g \) is odd and \( f \) is even, \( h = f \circ g \) is ALWAYS an EVEN function (not odd!).

Question 4
Prove or give a counter-example for each of the following statements:
(a) If \( f, g \) are increasing, so is their sum \( f + g \).
(b) If \( f, g \) are increasing, so is their product \( fg \).
Hint: For (a), sum basic inequalities. For (b), test negative function values.
Solution:

(a) True.

Let \( x_1 < x_2 \). Since \( f \) and \( g \) are increasing, \( f(x_1) \le f(x_2) \) and \( g(x_1) \le g(x_2) \).

Adding these inequalities yields: \( f(x_1) + g(x_1) \le f(x_2) + g(x_2) \implies (f+g)(x_1) \le (f+g)(x_2) \).

(b) False.

Counter-example: Let \( f(x) = x \) and \( g(x) = x \). Both functions are strictly increasing on \( \mathbb{R} \).

However, their product is \( (fg)(x) = x^2 \), which is strictly decreasing on \( (-\infty, 0) \). Thus, \( fg \) is not an increasing function on \( \mathbb{R} \).

Question 5
Let \( f : [a, b] \to \mathbb{R} \) be continuous and \( c_1, \dots, c_n \in [a, b] \). Show that there is a point \( c \in [a, b] \) such that: \[ f(c) = \frac{f(c_1) + \dots + f(c_n)}{n} \]
Hint: Bound the average between the absolute minimum \( m \) and absolute maximum \( M \) of \( f \) on \( [a, b] \), then apply the Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT).
Solution:

Since \( f \) is continuous on the compact interval \( [a, b] \), by the Extreme Value Theorem, \( f \) attains its absolute minimum \( m \) and maximum \( M \) on \( [a, b] \).

Thus, for each \( i \in \{1, \dots, n\} \): \( m \le f(c_i) \le M \).

Summing these inequalities over all \( i \):

\[ n \cdot m \le \sum_{i=1}^n f(c_i) \le n \cdot M \implies m \le \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n f(c_i) \le M \]

Let \( A = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n f(c_i) \). Since \( m \le A \le M \), by the Intermediate Value Theorem, there exists some \( c \in [a, b] \) such that \( f(c) = A \).

Question 6
Suppose \( f, g : [a, b] \to \mathbb{R} \) are continuous functions such that \( f(a) > g(a) \) and \( f(b) < g(b) \). Show that there is a \( c \in (a, b) \) such that \( f(c) = g(c) \).
Hint: Define an auxiliary function \( h(x) = f(x) - g(x) \) and evaluate \( h(a) \) and \( h(b) \).
Solution:

Define a function \( h : [a, b] \to \mathbb{R} \) by \( h(x) = f(x) - g(x) \).

  • Since \( f \) and \( g \) are continuous on \( [a, b] \), \( h \) is also continuous on \( [a, b] \).
  • At the endpoints: \[ h(a) = f(a) - g(a) > 0 \] \[ h(b) = f(b) - g(b) < 0 \]

Since \( h(a) > 0 \) and \( h(b) < 0 \), 0 lies strictly between \( h(a) \) and \( h(b) \). By the Intermediate Value Theorem, there exists a \( c \in (a, b) \) such that \( h(c) = 0 \), which implies \( f(c) = g(c) \).

Question 7
The following tasks will establish that a cubic polynomial \( p(x) = x^3 + ax^2 + bx + c \) (where \( a, b, c \in \mathbb{R} \)) has at least one real root.
(a) Show that for \( x \ge 1 \), \( p(x) \ge x^3 \left( 1 - \frac{|a| + |b| + |c|}{x} \right) \). Hence, deduce that there exists a point \( x_1 \) such that \( p(x_1) > 0 \).
(b) Show that for \( x \le -1 \), \( p(x) \le x^3 \left( 1 + \frac{|a| + |b| + |c|}{x} \right) \). Hence, deduce that there exists a point \( x_2 \) such that \( p(x_2) < 0 \).
Hint:

1. Factor out \( x^3 \) from \( p(x) \) to separate the leading dominant term from the remaining tail terms.

2. Apply the Triangle Inequality \( |u + v + w| \le |u| + |v| + |w| \) together with \( |u| \ge -u \).

3. Use the restriction \( |x| \ge 1 \) to simplify powers of \( x \) in denominators: \( |x|^k \ge |x| \) for all \( k \ge 1 \).

4. Choose \( x \) sufficiently large so that the expression inside the parentheses becomes strictly positive.

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:

Part (a): Analysis for \( x \ge 1 \)

Step 1: Express \( p(x) \) by factoring out \( x^3 \)

For any \( x \neq 0 \), we can factor out the dominant term \( x^3 \):

\[ p(x) = x^3 + ax^2 + bx + c = x^3 \left( 1 + \frac{a}{x} + \frac{b}{x^2} + \frac{c}{x^3} \right) \]

Step 2: Apply absolute value bounds to the tail terms

By the Triangle Inequality, the absolute value of the sum of the lower-order terms satisfies:

\[ \left| \frac{a}{x} + \frac{b}{x^2} + \frac{c}{x^3} \right| \le \left| \frac{a}{x} \right| + \left| \frac{b}{x^2} \right| + \left| \frac{c}{x^3} \right| = \frac{|a|}{|x|} + \frac{|b|}{|x|^2} + \frac{|c|}{|x|^3} \]

Step 3: Simplify using the condition \( x \ge 1 \)

Since \( x \ge 1 \), we have \( x = |x| \ge 1 \). This implies:

\[ x^2 \ge x \implies \frac{1}{x^2} \le \frac{1}{x} \quad \text{and} \quad x^3 \ge x \implies \frac{1}{x^3} \le \frac{1}{x} \]

Substituting these bounds back into our absolute value inequality gives:

\[ \left| \frac{a}{x} + \frac{b}{x^2} + \frac{c}{x^3} \right| \le \frac{|a|}{x} + \frac{|b|}{x} + \frac{|c|}{x} = \frac{|a| + |b| + |c|}{x} \]

Step 4: Derive lower bound for \( p(x) \)

For any real number \( y \), we know that \( y \ge -|y| \). Letting \( y = \frac{a}{x} + \frac{b}{x^2} + \frac{c}{x^3} \), we obtain:

\[ 1 + \frac{a}{x} + \frac{b}{x^2} + \frac{c}{x^3} \ge 1 - \left| \frac{a}{x} + \frac{b}{x^2} + \frac{c}{x^3} \right| \ge 1 - \frac{|a| + |b| + |c|}{x} \]

Since \( x \ge 1 \), \( x^3 > 0 \). Multiplying the entire inequality by \( x^3 \) preserves the inequality direction:

\[ p(x) = x^3 \left( 1 + \frac{a}{x} + \frac{b}{x^2} + \frac{c}{x^3} \right) \ge x^3 \left( 1 - \frac{|a| + |b| + |c|}{x} \right) \quad \blacksquare \]

Step 5: Existence of \( x_1 \) such that \( p(x_1) > 0 \)

To guarantee \( p(x_1) > 0 \), we need the factor \( 1 - \frac{|a| + |b| + |c|}{x} \) to be strictly positive.

Set \( 1 - \frac{|a| + |b| + |c|}{x} > 0 \implies x > |a| + |b| + |c| \).

Therefore, we explicitly choose:

\[ x_1 = \max\Big(1, \, |a| + |b| + |c| + 1\Big) \]

With this choice, \( x_1 \ge 1 \) and \( x_1 > |a| + |b| + |c| \), so:

\[ \frac{|a| + |b| + |c|}{x_1} < 1 \implies 1 - \frac{|a| + |b| + |c|}{x_1} > 0 \]

Since both \( x_1^3 > 0 \) and \( \left( 1 - \frac{|a| + |b| + |c|}{x_1} \right) > 0 \), we conclude:

\[ p(x_1) \ge x_1^3 \left( 1 - \frac{|a| + |b| + |c|}{x_1} \right) > 0 \]

Part (b): Analysis for \( x \le -1 \)

Step 1: Express \( p(x) \) for negative values of \( x \)

Again, factor out \( x^3 \):

\[ p(x) = x^3 \left( 1 + \frac{a}{x} + \frac{b}{x^2} + \frac{c}{x^3} \right) \]

Step 2: Simplify absolute value bounds for \( x \le -1 \)

When \( x \le -1 \), we have \( |x| = -x \ge 1 \). Thus \( |x|^2 = x^2 \ge |x| \) and \( |x|^3 = -x^3 \ge |x| \), which means:

\[ \frac{1}{x^2} \le \frac{1}{|x|} = -\frac{1}{x} \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{1}{|x|^3} \le \frac{1}{|x|} = -\frac{1}{x} \]

Applying the Triangle Inequality as before:

\[ \left| \frac{a}{x} + \frac{b}{x^2} + \frac{c}{x^3} \right| \le \frac{|a|}{|x|} + \frac{|b|}{|x|^2} + \frac{|c|}{|x|^3} \le \frac{|a| + |b| + |c|}{|x|} = \frac{|a| + |b| + |c|}{-x} \]

Step 3: Derive upper bound for \( p(x) \)

Using the property \( y \le |y| \):

\[ 1 + \frac{a}{x} + \frac{b}{x^2} + \frac{c}{x^3} \le 1 + \left| \frac{a}{x} + \frac{b}{x^2} + \frac{c}{x^3} \right| \le 1 + \frac{|a| + |b| + |c|}{-x} = 1 - \frac{|a| + |b| + |c|}{x} \]

Now, because \( x \le -1 \), the factor \( x^3 \) is negative (\( x^3 < 0 \)).

When multiplying an inequality by a negative number, the direction of the inequality reverses:

\[ p(x) = x^3 \left( 1 + \frac{a}{x} + \frac{b}{x^2} + \frac{c}{x^3} \right) \le x^3 \left( 1 + \frac{|a| + |b| + |c|}{x} \right) \quad \blacksquare \]

Step 4: Existence of \( x_2 \) such that \( p(x_2) < 0 \)

We want the upper bound to be strictly negative. Since \( x^3 < 0 \), we need the factor inside brackets to be strictly positive:

\[ 1 + \frac{|a| + |b| + |c|}{x} > 0 \implies \frac{|a| + |b| + |c|}{-x} < 1 \implies -x > |a| + |b| + |c| \]

Therefore, we explicitly choose:

\[ x_2 = \min\Big(-1, \, -(|a| + |b| + |c| + 1)\Big) \]

With this choice, \( x_2 \le -1 \) and \( -x_2 = |x_2| > |a| + |b| + |c| \). Thus:

\[ \frac{|a| + |b| + |c|}{-x_2} < 1 \implies 1 + \frac{|a| + |b| + |c|}{x_2} > 0 \]

Because \( x_2^3 < 0 \) and \( \left( 1 + \frac{|a| + |b| + |c|}{x_2} \right) > 0 \), their product is strictly negative:

\[ p(x_2) \le x_2^3 \left( 1 + \frac{|a| + |b| + |c|}{x_2} \right) < 0 \]

Conclusion (Existence of a Real Root):

Since \( p(x) \) is a polynomial, it is continuous everywhere on \( [x_2, x_1] \). Since \( p(x_2) < 0 \) and \( p(x_1) > 0 \), by the Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT), there must exist at least one real root \( x_0 \in (x_2, x_1) \) such that \( p(x_0) = 0 \).

Question 8
Show that the equation \( \cos x = x^3 \) has at least one solution.
Hint: Define \( f(x) = \cos x - x^3 \) and test values at \( x = 0 \) and \( x = 1 \).
Solution:

Let \( f(x) = \cos x - x^3 \). Note that \( f(x) \) is continuous on \( \mathbb{R} \).

Evaluate \( f \) at key test points:

  • \( f(0) = \cos(0) - 0^3 = 1 - 0 = 1 > 0 \)
  • \( f(1) = \cos(1) - 1^3 = \cos(1) - 1 < 0 \) (since \( \cos(1) < 1 \))

Since \( f(0) > 0 \) and \( f(1) < 0 \), by the Intermediate Value Theorem, there exists at least one solution \( c \in (0, 1) \) such that \( f(c) = 0 \), which implies \( \cos c = c^3 \).

Question 9
Let \( [x] \) be the greatest integer function. Prove the following Riemann integration identities:
(a) \( \int_{0}^{n} [x] \, dx = \frac{n(n - 1)}{2} \) for each \( n \in \mathbb{N} \).
(b) \( \int_{0}^{n^2} [\sqrt{x}] \, dx = \frac{n(n - 1)(4n + 1)}{6} \) for each \( n \in \mathbb{N} \).
Hint: Partition the domain into sub-intervals where the integrand is constant. For (b), split over intervals \( [k^2, (k+1)^2) \).
Solution:

(a) Split the integral into unit length intervals \( [k, k+1) \), where \( [x] = k \):

\[ \int_{0}^{n} [x] \, dx = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \int_{k}^{k+1} k \, dx = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} k (1) = \frac{n(n-1)}{2} \]

(b) Split the integral over intervals \( [k^2, (k+1)^2) \) where \( [\sqrt{x}] = k \):

\[ \int_{0}^{n^2} [\sqrt{x}] \, dx = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \int_{k^2}^{(k+1)^2} k \, dx \]

The length of each interval is \( (k+1)^2 - k^2 = 2k + 1 \). Thus:

\[ = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} k(2k + 1) = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} (2k^2 + k) = 2\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} k^2 + \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} k \]

Applying standard summation formulas:

\[ = 2 \left[ \frac{(n-1)n(2n-1)}{6} \right] + \frac{(n-1)n}{2} = \frac{n(n-1)(4n + 1)}{6} \]
Question 10
Let \( H(x) \) denote the Heaviside (unit step) function. Compute the following definite integrals:
(a) \( \int_{-1}^{2} \big(H(x) + 2H(x - 1)\big) \, dx \)
(b) \( \int_{-1}^{2} \big(H(1 - x) - H(x - 1)\big) \, dx \)
Hint:

1. Recall that the Heaviside step function is defined as \( H(u) = 1 \) for \( u \ge 0 \) and \( H(u) = 0 \) for \( u < 0 \).

2. Break the interval of integration \( [-1, 2] \) at the transition points (where the arguments of \( H \) change sign, i.e., \( x = 0 \) and \( x = 1 \)).

3. Evaluate the constant values of the integrands on each piece-wise sub-interval before computing the integral using area or antiderivatives.

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:

1. Definition and Graph of the Heaviside Function

The Heaviside function (or unit step function), denoted by \( H(x) \), is defined as:

\[ H(x) = \begin{cases} 1, & \text{if } x \ge 0 \\ 0, & \text{if } x < 0 \end{cases} \]

Graph of \( H(x) \):

  • For \( x \in (-\infty, 0) \), the function value is constant at \( y = 0 \).
  • At \( x = 0 \), there is a jump discontinuity from \( 0 \) to \( 1 \).
  • For \( x \in [0, \infty) \), the function value is constant at \( y = 1 \).

Shifted and Reflected Heaviside Functions:

  • \( H(x - 1) = \begin{cases} 1, & \text{if } x - 1 \ge 0 \implies x \ge 1 \\ 0, & \text{if } x - 1 < 0 \implies x < 1 \end{cases} \)
  • \( H(1 - x) = \begin{cases} 1, & \text{if } 1 - x \ge 0 \implies x \le 1 \\ 0, & \text{if } 1 - x < 0 \implies x > 1 \end{cases} \)

Part (a): Evaluate \( \int_{-1}^{2} \big(H(x) + 2H(x - 1)\big) \, dx \)

Step 1: Identify key transition points

The integrands \( H(x) \) and \( H(x-1) \) change values at \( x = 0 \) and \( x = 1 \). Thus, we split the domain \( [-1, 2] \) into three sub-intervals:

\[ [-1, 2] = [-1, 0) \cup [0, 1) \cup [1, 2] \]

Step 2: Determine integrand values on each sub-interval

  • On \( [-1, 0) \): \( x < 0 \) and \( x < 1 \). \[ H(x) = 0, \quad H(x-1) = 0 \implies H(x) + 2H(x-1) = 0 + 2(0) = 0 \]
  • On \( [0, 1) \): \( x \ge 0 \) and \( x < 1 \). \[ H(x) = 1, \quad H(x-1) = 0 \implies H(x) + 2H(x-1) = 1 + 2(0) = 1 \]
  • On \( [1, 2] \): \( x \ge 0 \) and \( x \ge 1 \). \[ H(x) = 1, \quad H(x-1) = 1 \implies H(x) + 2H(x-1) = 1 + 2(1) = 3 \]

Step 3: Split and compute the integral

\[ \int_{-1}^{2} \big(H(x) + 2H(x - 1)\big) \, dx = \int_{-1}^{0} 0 \, dx + \int_{0}^{1} 1 \, dx + \int_{1}^{2} 3 \, dx \] \[ = 0 + 1 \cdot (1 - 0) + 3 \cdot (2 - 1) = 1 + 3 = 4 \quad \blacksquare \]

Part (b): Evaluate \( \int_{-1}^{2} \big(H(1 - x) - H(x - 1)\big) \, dx \)

Step 1: Identify key transition points

Both \( H(1-x) \) and \( H(x-1) \) switch states at \( x = 1 \). Therefore, we split the interval \( [-1, 2] \) at \( x = 1 \):

\[ [-1, 2] = [-1, 1) \cup [1, 2] \]

Step 2: Determine integrand values on each sub-interval

  • On \( [-1, 1) \): \( x < 1 \). \[ H(1-x) = 1, \quad H(x-1) = 0 \implies H(1-x) - H(x-1) = 1 - 0 = 1 \]
  • On \( [1, 2] \): \( x \ge 1 \). \[ H(1-x) = 0 \quad (\text{since } 1-x \le 0), \quad H(x-1) = 1 \implies H(1-x) - H(x-1) = 0 - 1 = -1 \]

Step 3: Split and compute the integral

\[ \int_{-1}^{2} \big(H(1 - x) - H(x - 1)\big) \, dx = \int_{-1}^{1} 1 \, dx + \int_{1}^{2} (-1) \, dx \] \[ = 1 \cdot (1 - (-1)) + (-1) \cdot (2 - 1) = 1 \cdot 2 + (-1) \cdot 1 = 2 - 1 = 1 \quad \blacksquare \]
