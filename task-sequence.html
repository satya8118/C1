Tier 1 Sets and mappings — basics

  1. 1State the domain of \(f\).
  2. 2Propose a natural co-domain (usually \(\mathbb{R}\)), then compute the range (image) of \(f\).
  3. 3Compare the range to the co-domain directly — is \(f\) onto its stated co-domain?
  4. 4For a given subset \(A\) of the domain (an interval or finite set of points), compute \(f(A)\), the image of \(A\).
  5. 5For a given \(y\) in the co-domain, find \(f^{-1}(\{y\})\) — how many pre-images does it have? (Zero, one, or several.)
  6. 6For a subset \(B\) of the co-domain, find \(f^{-1}(B)\).
  7. 7Verify \(f(f^{-1}(B)) \subseteq B\); find an example (if any) where the containment is strict.
  8. 8Verify \(A \subseteq f^{-1}(f(A))\); explain why equality here is closely tied to injectivity.

Tier 2 Injective, surjective, bijective

  1. 9Determine whether \(f\) is one-one on its full domain (algebraic test: \(f(x_1)=f(x_2)\Rightarrow x_1=x_2\)).
  2. 10If not, find the largest interval containing a given point on which \(f\) is one-one.
  3. 11Determine whether \(f\) is onto its co-domain.
  4. 12State whether \(f\) is a bijection on its full domain; if not, state the restricted domain/co-domain pair on which it becomes one.

Tier 3 Inverse

  1. 13On the interval from Item 10, find a formula for \(f^{-1}\); state its domain and range.
  2. 14Verify \(f(f^{-1}(x))=x\) and \(f^{-1}(f(x))=x\) on the appropriate domains.
  3. 15Graph \(f\) and \(f^{-1}\) together with the line \(y=x\).

Tier 4 Algebra of \(f\) with itself

  1. 16Compute \((f+f)(x) = 2f(x)\).
  2. 17Compute \((f+f+f)(x) = 3f(x)\); generalize to \(nf(x)\).
  3. 18Compute \((f-f)(x)\) (trivially 0) versus \(f(x)-f(-x)\) — explain why these are different.
  4. 19Compute \((f\cdot f)(x)=f(x)^2\) and \((f\cdot f\cdot f)(x) = f(x)^3\).
  5. 20Compute \(f(x)+f^{-1}(x)\) and \(f(x)\cdot f^{-1}(x)\) wherever both are defined (on the restricted domain from Items 10/13).

Tier 5 Composition

  1. 21Compute \((f\circ f)(x)=f(f(x))\); state its domain carefully — it may shrink relative to \(f\)'s own domain.
  2. 22Compute \((f\circ f\circ f)(x)\); note how the domain shrinks further.
  3. 23Compute \((f\circ f^{-1})(x)\) and \((f^{-1}\circ f)(x)\) explicitly and reconcile with Item 14.
  4. 24Find all fixed points: solve \(f(x)=x\). Interpret graphically as intersections with \(y=x\).

Tier 6 Limits

  1. 25Compute \(\lim_{x\to x_0} f(x)\) at a specified interior point \(x_0\), using limit laws.
  2. 26Compute one-sided limits at any boundary or singular point of the domain, and state whether the two-sided limit exists there.
  3. 27Compute \(\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)\) and \(\lim_{x\to-\infty}f(x)\) where the domain permits; identify horizontal asymptotes.
  4. 28If \(f\) has a vertical asymptote, compute the one-sided limits (\(\pm\infty\)) there.

Tier 7 Continuity and \(\varepsilon\text{–}\delta\)

  1. 29For the point \(x_0\) in Item 25 and a specific \(\varepsilon\) (e.g. \(\varepsilon=0.1\)), find a \(\delta\) that works in the \(\varepsilon\text{–}\delta\) definition.
  2. 30Find the maximum possible \(\delta\) for that \(\varepsilon\) — solve the resulting inequality exactly rather than settling for a workable but non-optimal bound.
  3. 31Write out the full \(\varepsilon\text{–}\delta\) proof that \(f\) is continuous at \(x_0\), using the exact \(\delta(\varepsilon)\) relationship found in Item 30.
  4. 32Discuss continuity at any special/singular point in the domain (removable, jump, or none, depending on the function).
  5. 33State the full set of points where \(f\) is continuous.
  6. 34If applicable, discuss whether \(f\) can be continuously extended to a point outside its stated domain (a removable-discontinuity case).

Tier 8 Uniform continuity and classical existence theorems

  1. 35Discuss uniform continuity of \(f\) on a specified closed, bounded sub-interval of its domain.
  2. 36Verify the Intermediate Value Theorem numerically: pick \(a,b\) with \(f(a),f(b)\) straddling a target value, and locate the guaranteed point.
  3. 37Verify the Extreme Value Theorem on a closed interval: does \(f\) attain a max and min there, and where?
  4. 38Discuss Lipschitz continuity: is there an \(L\) with \(|f(x)-f(y)|\le L|x-y|\) on a given interval?

Tier 9 Differentiability, deeper

  1. 39Compute \(f'(x)\) directly from the first-principles limit definition at a specified point (not just by differentiation rules).
  2. 40At any corner/cusp point identified earlier, compute the left-hand and right-hand derivatives separately and confirm they disagree.
  3. 41Compute \(f''(x)\) and \(f'''(x)\); where a pattern exists (exponential, trig, log families), conjecture and prove a formula for \(f^{(n)}(x)\).
  4. 42If the assigned function is of the oscillatory type (e.g. \(x^2\sin(a/x)\)), show \(f\) is differentiable at the special point but \(f'\) is discontinuous there.

Tier 10 Mean Value Theorems

  1. 43Verify Rolle's Theorem on an interval \([a,b]\) with \(f(a)=f(b)\); find the guaranteed point \(c\) with \(f'(c)=0\).
  2. 44Verify the Lagrange Mean Value Theorem on \([a,b]\); find \(c\) with \(f'(c)=\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}\), and interpret it as "tangent parallel to chord."
  3. 45Apply Cauchy's Mean Value Theorem to \(f\) and a second function \(g(x)=x^2\) (or another convenient \(g\)) on an interval.
  4. 46Use the Mean Value Theorem to prove a bound: if \(|f'(x)|\le M\) on an interval, show \(|f(x_1)-f(x_2)|\le M|x_1-x_2|\) there.

Tier 11 Curve tracing (synthesis)

  1. 47Classify symmetry: even, odd, or neither; periodic or not.
  2. 48Find all asymptotes precisely — vertical, horizontal, and oblique/slant (via polynomial division where \(f\) is rational).
  3. 49Assemble a complete curve-tracing summary combining domain, intercepts, symmetry, asymptotes, monotonicity, concavity, and inflection points into one final annotated sketch.

Tier 12 Indeterminate forms

  1. 50Identify any indeterminate limit arising from \(f\) (e.g. \(f(x)/x\) as \(x\to0\), or \(f(x)^{1/x}\) as \(x\to\infty\)) and evaluate it with L'Hôpital's Rule, checking the hypotheses first.

Tier 13 Riemann integration

  1. 51Partition an interval \([a,b]\) into \(n\) equal subintervals; compute the upper sum \(U(f,P)\) and lower sum \(L(f,P)\) for a specific small \(n\) (e.g. \(n=4\)).
  2. 52Show \(U(f,P)-L(f,P)\to 0\) as \(n\to\infty\), and use this to argue \(f\) is Riemann integrable on \([a,b]\).
  3. 53Compute the Riemann sum using left endpoints, right endpoints, and midpoints for a chosen \(n\); compare all three to the exact value of \(\int_a^b f(x)\,dx\).
  4. 54Evaluate \(\int_a^b f(x)\,dx\) via the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (or numerically, stating why, if \(f\) has no elementary antiderivative).
  5. 55Compute the average value of \(f\) on \([a,b]\), \(\frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b f(x)\,dx\), and find a point where \(f\) equals that average (Mean Value Theorem for integrals).

Tier 14 Length, volume, and surface of revolution

  1. 56Set up and evaluate the arc length \(\int_a^b\sqrt{1+f'(x)^2}\,dx\) over a chosen interval.
  2. 57Volume by disks/washers: rotate the region under \(y=f(x)\) about the \(x\)-axis and evaluate \(V=\pi\int_a^b f(x)^2\,dx\).
  3. 58Volume by shells: rotate the same region about the \(y\)-axis (restrict to \(x\ge0\) if needed) and evaluate \(V=2\pi\int_a^b x\,f(x)\,dx\).
  4. 59Surface area of revolution about the \(x\)-axis: \(S=2\pi\int_a^b f(x)\sqrt{1+f'(x)^2}\,dx\).
  5. 60Repeat the volume or surface computation rotating about a shifted axis (e.g. \(y=c\) or \(x=c\)) instead of the coordinate axis.

Tier 15 A few more single-variable staples

  1. 61Find the degree-2 (or 3) Taylor polynomial of \(f\) at a point \(x_0\); estimate the error using the Lagrange remainder.
  2. 62Apply one or two iterations of Newton's Method to approximate a zero of \(f\) starting from a given \(x_0\).
  3. 63If the natural interval of integration is infinite or \(f\) is singular at an endpoint, discuss convergence/divergence of the resulting improper integral.
