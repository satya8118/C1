Arc Length

Length of a Smooth Curve

Motivation: Polygon Approximation

By dividing an interval $[a,b]$ into $n$ subintervals, we approximate the curve with straight line segments. Using the Mean Value Theorem, each segment length $dL$ is:

$$dL = \sqrt{dx^2 + dy^2} = \sqrt{1 + [f'(x)]^2} \, dx$$

Taking the limit as $n \to \infty$ yields the integral:

$$L = \int_a^b \sqrt{1 + [f'(x)]^2} \, dx$$

Benchmark: Unit Quarter-Circle

For $y = \sqrt{1-x^2}$ on $[0,1]$, $f'(x) = \frac{-x}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$. The integrand simplifies to $\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$.

$$L = \int_0^1 \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} \, dx = \left[ \arcsin(x) \right]_0^1 = \frac{\pi}{2}$$

This confirms $C = 2\pi r \implies C/4 = \pi/2$.

Area Between Curves

2D Bounded Area

Motivation: Representative Strip

We slice the region bounded by two curves into thin vertical rectangles of width $dx$.

  • Height: $f(x) - g(x)$
  • Width: $dx$
  • Differential Area $dA$: $[f(x) - g(x)]dx$
$$A = \int_a^b [f(x) - g(x)] \, dx$$

Interactive Lab: Riemann Strips

$$A = \int_0^1 (x^2 - x^3) dx = \left[\frac{x^3}{3} - \frac{x^4}{4}\right]_0^1 = \frac{1}{12}$$

Surface Area

Surface of Revolution (x-axis)

Motivation: Sweeping a Band

Rotate a tiny arc segment $ds$ around the x-axis. It sweeps out a circular ribbon (frustum) in 3D.

  • Radius: $r = f(x)$
  • Circumference: $C = 2\pi f(x)$
  • Band Width: $ds = \sqrt{1+[f'(x)]^2}dx$
  • Differential Surface $dS$: $C \times ds$
$$S = \int_a^b 2\pi f(x) \sqrt{1 + [f'(x)]^2} \, dx$$

Volume of Revolution

Volume by Disk Method

Strictly focusing on solids formed by regions adjacent to the axis of rotation.

Motivation: The Solid Disk

Rotate a vertical rectangle (width $dx$, height $f(x)$) around the x-axis. It forms a solid coin-like cylinder (a disk).

  • Disk Radius: $R = f(x)$
  • Face Area: $A = \pi R^2 = \pi [f(x)]^2$
  • Thickness: $dx$
  • Differential Volume $dV$: $A \times dx$
$$V = \pi \int_a^b [f(x)]^2 \, dx$$

Interactive Lab: Disk Convergence ($y = \sqrt{x}$)

n = 10

Radius is $y$. Squaring removes the square root naturally:

$$V = \pi \int_{-r}^r (\sqrt{r^2 - x^2})^2 dx = \pi \left[ r^2x - \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_{-r}^r = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$$
